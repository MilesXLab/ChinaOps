# 🍽️ Food Allergies & Dietary Restrictions SOP

<div class="plain-summary">
  <strong class="plain-summary-label">Plain English</strong>
  <p>Carry a written allergy card in Chinese. Show it before ordering; do not rely on English menus alone.</p>
  <p>When unsure, choose simpler dishes or cook at the hotel.</p>
  <p><span class="scope-badge">Scope: national</span></p>

**TL;DR:** Always use a printed **Allergy Card** in Chinese. Prefer **清炒 / 清蒸** dishes. For anaphylaxis, use your **EpiPen**, call **120**, and route to a major hospital (in Shanghai: Jiahui / United Family when possible).

**Prerequisites:**
- **Printed Allergy Card:** Laminated, postcard-size, your allergens in Chinese characters (carry several copies).
- **EpiPen / Antihistamine:** In carry-on, not checked bags.
- **Translation app:** Google Translate camera mode or Pleco offline packs.

---

## 📋 The Runbook

### 1. Pre-order (use the card first)
- Hand the **Allergy Card** to the server *before* they take the order.
- Point to allergens and say: **我对这些过敏** (*Wǒ duì zhèxiē guòmǐn*).
- Prefer dishes labeled **清炒** (clear stir-fry) or **清蒸** (steamed).
- **Verification:** Ask **这里面都没有这些吗？** (*Zhè lǐmiàn dōu méiyǒu zhèxiē ma?*) and wait for a clear yes.

### 2. Backup if the card is ignored
1. **Translation app:** Type the restriction, show the Chinese text, ask them to read it back.
2. **Simple phrases** (also useful for quick meals):

| Restriction | Chinese | Pinyin |
|------------|---------|--------|
| No nuts | 不要坚果 | búyào jiānguǒ |
| No peanuts | 不要花生 | búyào huāshēng |
| No shellfish | 不要虾/蟹 | búyào xiā / xiè |
| No fish | 不要鱼 | búyào yú |
| No dairy | 不要牛奶 | búyào niúnǎi |
| No eggs | 不要鸡蛋 | búyào jīdàn |
| Vegetarian | 素食 | sùshí |
| Boiled, no sauce | 清炒，没有酱 | qīngchǎo, méiyǒu jiàng |
| Plain (little oil) | 清淡（不要油） | qīngdàn (búyào yóu) |

### 3. Sauce and oil (hidden allergens)
- **Peanut oil (花生油)** and **oyster sauce (蚝油)** are common hidden risks.
- Request **corn oil (玉米油)** or **sunflower oil (葵花籽油)** when possible.
- For severe allergies: **不要酱** (*bùyào jiàng* — no sauce) and ask for a clean pan/wok.

### 4. Before the first bite
- [ ] Visual check for nuts/seeds
- [ ] Shellfish smell check if relevant
- [ ] If unsure, **do not eat** — fall back to trusted chains or hotel food

### 5. Safer restaurant choices
| Type | Why | Notes |
|------|-----|-------|
| Vegetarian Buddhist | Plant-based default | Still confirm no fish sauce |
| Japanese | Ingredient culture | Plain grilled options |
| Steakhouse / Western | Simpler build | English menus common |
| International hotel | Staff training | Can customize |
| **Avoid:** street stalls, buffets, shared-oil family kitchens | No control | High cross-contamination |

### 6. Grocery shopping
1. **City Super / Ole' / premium malls** — English labels, familiar brands (premium price).
2. **Carrefour / Auchan** — check labels carefully.
3. **JD / Freshippo delivery** — order to hotel; read ingredient text.
4. Carry backup snacks: nut-free bars, plain crackers, shelf-stable milk alternative (verify labels).

## 🚨 Fallback (Plan B)

### Mild reaction (hives / itch)
1. Stop eating · water · your antihistamine · monitor 30 minutes.
2. If worsening → treat as severe.

### Severe reaction (swelling / breathing)
1. **EpiPen** if prescribed · call **120**.
2. Shout: **救命！过敏了！** (*Jiùmìng! Guòmǐn le!*).
3. Show the Chinese allergy card at hospital triage.
4. Prefer major ER / international-friendly hospitals when reachable (Shanghai: Jiahui, United Family).

## 💡 TechDad's Tips

- Laminate **5–10** allergy cards; hand one out every meal.
- For severe cases, ask to speak to the **chef**, not only the server.
- Malls (e.g. Xintiandi, Jing'an Kerry) staff handle dietary requests better.
- Screenshot menus of known-safe dishes for the next visit.
- Keep emergency meds on your person, not in a checked stroller bag.
- MSG discomfort ≠ life-threatening allergy — keep the card focused on critical allergens.

## FAQ

**Q: Is street food safe with allergies?**
A: Generally no. Ingredient control and communication are poor.

**Q: Can I trust “allergy-free” menu labels?**
A: Not fully — confirm with kitchen; oil cross-contamination is common.

**Q: Can I bring my own food for a child?**
A: Usually yes; some places charge a small plate fee (~¥5–10).

**Q: Apps for allergens in Chinese dishes?**
A: No single restaurant allergy app is reliable. Use your card + Translate camera + Pleco. For packaged food, barcode apps (e.g. Yuka where available) help.
