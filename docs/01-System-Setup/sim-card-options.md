**TL;DR:** For 100% "Firewall Bypass," use a **Travel eSIM** (Trip.com, Holafly, Simify, or Nomad). For local services (receiving SMS, booking Didi, or registering at local hotspots), you need a **Physical SIM** from China Unicom or China Telecom. 

**Prerequisites:**
- **Unlocked Phone:** Crucial. Check with your home carrier before departure.
- **Physical ID:** Passport (required for local SIM registration at physical stores).
- **Setup Time:** 15 mins (eSIM) vs 45 mins (Store visit).

---

## 📋 The Runbook

### 1. The eSIM "Bypass" Protocol
- **Step 1:** Purchase a **Travel eSIM** via **Trip.com**, **Holafly**, **Simify**, or **Nomad** *before* landing.
- **Step 2:** Choose the plan that fits your data needs (Trip.com is typically the cheapest for data; Holafly offers unlimited data). These eSIMs route traffic through HK/Macau/Singapore, bypassing the Great Firewall natively.
- **Verification:** Switch your data connection to the eSIM. Open a blocked website (e.g., `google.com`). If it loads, you are "off-wall."

### 2. The Local SIM "Full-Stack"
- **Step 1:** Go to a **China Unicom (中国联通)** or **China Telecom (中国电信)** official service center (avoid small street kiosks; they often cannot process foreign passports).
- **Step 2:** Ask for the **"Short-term Tourist SIM"** (usually 100-200 RMB). Present your physical passport.
- **Step 3:** The staff will perform a mandatory face-scan for registration.
- **Verification:** Reboot your phone. Verify you receive local SMS and can see the 4G/5G signal icon.

### 3. Comparison of Connectivity Nodes

| Option Type | Popular Providers | Best For | VPN Required? | SMS Verification (+86)? | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Travel eSIM** | Trip.com, Holafly, Simify, Nomad | Short-to-medium stays (7-21 days) | **No** (Native Bypass) | ❌ No (Data-only or non-Chinese number) | Best for social media/maps; cannot register for some local apps requiring +86 numbers. |
| **Local Physical SIM** | China Unicom, China Telecom | Long stays (1 month+), heavy local app usage | **YES** (Firewall active) | **Full Support** (Provides local +86 number) | Mandatory face scan; enables registration for Didi, Meituan, and local public Wi-Fi. |
| **Home Roaming** | T-Mobile, Vodafone, Telstra | Short business trips | **No** (Native Bypass) | Varies (Keeps home number active) | Very expensive, but keeps your primary phone lines active. |

## 🚨 Fallback (Plan B)

### If your eSIM fails to activate:
1. **Airport Kiosks:** PVG and SHA arrivals have dedicated "Tourist SIM" kiosks. They are 2x the price of a city store but have English-speaking staff and will troubleshoot your settings on the spot.
2. **The "Hotspot" Patch:** If only one member of the family has a working SIM, enable **Personal Hotspot**. Note: This drains batteries fast—carry your power bank.
3. **Wi-Fi + VPN:** Use the airport/hotel Wi-Fi. It will likely require an SMS code. Providing your foreign country code (e.g., +1, +44) usually works for these portals.

## 💡 TechDad's Tips

- **The "Data Only" Trap:** Some eSIMs give you data but **no phone number**. Without a number (starting with +86), you cannot register for **Didi** or **Meituan**. Check the fine print.
- **China Mobile:** Unless your phone specifically supports TDD-LTE/TD-SCDMA (most modern iPhones do, but some older Androids don't), stick to **China Unicom**. They use a more global standard (GMS).
- **The "Verification" King:** If you plan to use local apps heavily, the +86 phone number is more valuable than the data itself. Use the +86 for the apps and your eSIM for the actual internet.

---

## 🚩 Strategic Gap: The "SPOF" Warning
**Single Point of Failure:** Relying on a data-only eSIM for a 3-week family trip. 
- **Hotfix:** Get a local physical SIM for the primary traveler (+86 number for bookings/Didi) and travel eSIMs for the rest of the family.
