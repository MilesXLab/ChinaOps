# 💊 Prescription Medication & Chronic Care SOP

**TL;DR:** Most prescription medications require a **hospital prescription** in China. International hospitals (Jiahui, United Family) can write prescriptions for common medications. Controlled substances (ADHD meds, opioids) may require special permits. Always bring a 3-month supply and a doctor's note from home.

**Prerequisites:**
- **Home Prescription:** Original prescription or doctor's note (in English, with generic drug names).
- **Medical Records:** Summary of your condition and current medications.
- **3-Month Supply:** Bring enough medication for your entire trip (recommended).
- **Insurance Card:** Both digital and physical copies.

---

## 📋 The Runbook

### 1. Pre-Travel Preparation
- **Step 1:** Before leaving home, obtain:
  - A **doctor's letter** explaining your condition and medications (in English)
  - A **3-month supply** of medications (if possible)
  - **Generic drug names** (not brand names) for all medications
- **Step 2:** Research if your medications are available in China:
  - Check if the generic name exists in Chinese pharmacies
  - Some medications may have different brand names
- **Verification:** Ensure you have enough medication to last your entire trip plus 2 weeks buffer.

### 2. Getting a Prescription (International Hospitals)
- **Step 1:** Make an appointment at an **international hospital** (Jiahui, United Family, Parkway Health).
- **Step 2:** Bring:
  - Your home prescription/doctor's note
  - Medical records (if available)
  - Your current medication bottles
- **Step 3:** The doctor will review your condition and write a Chinese prescription.
- **Verification:** Confirm the prescription includes:
  - Generic drug name (in English/Pinyin)
  - Dosage and frequency
  - Duration (usually 1-3 months)

### 3. Filling the Prescription
- **Step 1:** Take the prescription to the **hospital pharmacy** (usually in the same building).
- **Step 2:** If the hospital pharmacy doesn't have your medication, they can direct you to an external pharmacy.
- **Step 3:** Some medications may require **pre-authorization** from insurance.
- **Verification:** Check the medication name, dosage, and expiration date before leaving the pharmacy.

---

## 🚨 Fallback (Plan B)

### If your medication is not available or requires special permits:
1. **The "Mail Order" Option:** Some medications can be mailed from your home country, but check Chinese customs regulations. Controlled substances are usually prohibited.
2. **Alternative Medications:** Ask the doctor if there's a Chinese equivalent or alternative medication that works similarly.
3. **Telemedicine:** Some international clinics offer **video consultations** with doctors from your home country who can write prescriptions (may require special arrangements).
4. **Embassy Medical Services:** Some embassies maintain lists of English-speaking doctors who can help with prescription refills.

---

## 💡 TechDad's Tips

- **The "3-Month Rule":** Always bring a 3-month supply of medications. Prescription refills in China can be time-consuming and may require multiple hospital visits.
- **Generic Names are Key:** Chinese pharmacies use generic drug names (e.g., "Metformin" not "Glucophage"). Bring a list of generic names for all your medications.
- **Controlled Substances:** Medications like ADHD drugs (Adderall, Ritalin), opioids, or benzodiazepines may require special permits or may be unavailable. Check with your embassy or a Chinese hospital before traveling.
- **The "Insurance Pre-Authorization":** Some medications require insurance pre-authorization. International hospitals can help with this process, but it may take 1-2 days.

## 🔄 Common Medication Categories

### Over-the-Counter (Available Without Prescription)
- **Pain Relievers:** Ibuprofen (布洛芬), Paracetamol (对乙酰氨基酚)
- **Antihistamines:** Cetirizine (西替利嗪)
- **Antacids:** Omeprazole (奥美拉唑)
- **Cold Medicine:** Common cold remedies are widely available

### Prescription Required (Common)
- **Antibiotics:** Amoxicillin, Azithromycin (require hospital prescription)
- **Blood Pressure:** Most common BP medications are available
- **Diabetes:** Metformin, Insulin (available with prescription)
- **Asthma:** Inhalers (available with prescription)

### Controlled Substances (Special Permits May Be Required)
- **ADHD Medications:** Adderall, Ritalin (may be unavailable or require special permit)
- **Opioids:** Codeine, Tramadol (strictly controlled)
- **Benzodiazepines:** Xanax, Valium (may require special permit)

---

## 🚩 Strategic Gap: The "SPOF" Warning
**Single Point of Failure:** Running out of critical medications without a refill plan.
- **Hotfix:** Always maintain a 3-month supply and a doctor's note. Research medication availability in China before traveling. Have a backup plan (international hospital appointment) ready before you need it.
